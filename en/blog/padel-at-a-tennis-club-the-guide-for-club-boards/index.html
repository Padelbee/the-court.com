Padel at a tennis club: the guide for club boards

Padel on your own grounds, without your own investment: lease payment, space requirements, tasks and member benefits at a glance.

In short: With the joint venture between THE COURT and the Motions Racket Club (MRC), your tennis club gets padel on its own grounds without investing itself. Your club provides the space in return for a lease payment; the joint venture builds, finances and operates the facility. As of: November 2026.

Why is padel worthwhile for tennis clubs?

At many clubs, tennis courts stand empty: fewer teams, fewer members, the same maintenance costs. A court that is not played on only costs money. Padel brings new target groups to the facility: younger players, families and companies. That means more life in the clubhouse and the catering.

Padel and tennis are not competitors. People who discover padel often stay with tennis too.

How many padel courts fit on a tennis court?

Up to two padel courts fit on one tennis court. A padel court needs considerably less space than a tennis court. Whether your courts are suitable is something we check on site, together with planning law, noise and lighting.

Who does what in the joint venture?

A joint venture only works with clear roles. THE COURT develops the project, MRC operates the facility.

The joint venture takes care of

  • construction and financing of the courts
  • ongoing operation
  • youth and training programmes
  • tournaments at discounted rates
  • the brand "Motions Racket Club [location]" with co-branding for your club

Your club takes care of

  • the space, in return for a lease payment
  • maintenance of outdoor areas and courts
  • the sanitary facilities
  • anchoring padel in club life

That way your club remains the host, but not the entrepreneur. The club board does not have to run a new company.

What do the members get out of it?

  • 30% discount on the hourly rate, at all MRC locations, not just at home
  • Book 24 hours earlier than everyone else
  • More on offer: Access to events, training and partner discounts
  • Youth and training: Programmes for children and beginners

That way padel is not a foreign body at your facility, but part of your club. And a strong argument for new members. More in 30% discount: what your members get from the Motions Racket Club.

What should the club board clarify in advance?

  • Planning law: What does the zoning plan allow? On existing sports facilities this is often easier than on a greenfield site.
  • Noise: Padel is louder than tennis. Distance to residential buildings, court orientation and playing times belong in the concept early on.
  • Lighting: Floodlights extend playing time and need careful planning.
  • Members: Bring the board and members on board early. The member benefits are a strong argument here.
  • Tax and legal matters: Always clarify tax and legal questions with your tax advisor.

Our tip: get the municipality round the table early as well. More on this in Permits without headaches.
