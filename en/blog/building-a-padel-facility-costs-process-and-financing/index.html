Building a padel facility: costs, process and financing

The guide for operators, clubs and hotels: what a padel facility costs, how your project runs and how to finance it.

In short: A padel facility consists of three major cost blocks: the court from €27,000 net per court, the foundation with a guideline figure of around €35,000 and, if you want to play all year round, a hall from €280/m² net. On top of that come planning, permits, lighting and digitalisation. As of: November 2026.

Which padel court suits your budget?

The three court variants differ mainly in the struts between the glass elements. Every variant is available as a double court or a single court.

VariantFeatureSuits
Standardadditional struts, particularly sturdyclubs, outdoor facilities, focus on value for money
Panoramicfewer struts, larger glass surfacesclubs with spectators
Full Panoramicalmost continuous glass, minimal steel frameindoor clubs, tournaments, TV broadcasts

Our rule of thumb: where spectators stand, Panoramic pays off. Where wind and weather take their toll, Standard scores. We work with five manufacturers and compare quality, delivery time, manufacturer's warranty and price for you. You can find all variants and manufacturers on the padel courts page.

Fixed foundation or mobile platform?

The choice depends on the location and on the term of your rental or lease agreement. Both options are in a similar price range.

  • Fixed foundation: Concrete slab on a strip foundation, standard 40 × 80 cm, frost-proof and adapted to the soil. For locations that will stay for many years.
  • Mobile platform: Modular substructure without concrete, can be dismantled and relocated. For short leases, interim uses, car park decks or commercial sites. It can often be leased, but is currently not eligible in many funding programmes; we check this case by case.

What the structure and surface look like is explained in The foundation: invisible but crucial and in Mobile platform: padel for a limited time.

How does a padel project run?

Between the first idea and the first serve lie many decisions. We support you in seven stages:

  1. Consulting: We assess the space, target group and potential of your location.
  2. Financing: Purchase, leasing or joint venture – we find the model that suits you.
  3. Planning & Architecture: Courts, hall, lighting, paths, sanitary facilities. Everything coordinated, everything ready for approval.
  4. Construction: We deliver with more than 30 partners from planning, court and hall construction.
  5. Digitalisation: Booking, access, lighting and vending machines work hand in hand.
  6. Operation: If you wish, we also support you after the opening.
  7. Franchise: If you want a strong brand, you get a proven concept.

Where do padel projects get stuck in the permit process?

The court is ordered quickly; the permit often takes longer. You should clarify three issues early:

  • Location and planning law: What does the zoning plan allow on your site? On existing sports facilities this is often easier than on a greenfield site.
  • Noise: Padel is louder than tennis. Distance to residential buildings, court orientation and playing times belong in the concept early on.
  • Lighting: We include light poles, colour temperature and alignment in the planning from the start.

Our tip: get the municipality round the table early. More on this in Permits without headaches.

For vending machines, there is leasing from €109 and hire purchase from €104 a month, for example with a 48-month term and a 20% final instalment, subject to credit approval. More on this in Financing padel: purchase, leasing or joint venture?

Important: Always clarify tax and accounting questions with your tax advisor.

What do you need for operation?

The biggest cost factor in operation is often the staff. Vending machines rent out rackets and sell balls, grips, snacks and drinks around the clock. Smart Access switches lighting and access with the booking, so nobody needs a key. You can find all models and prices on the vending machines page.
